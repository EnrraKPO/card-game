Mutation System Design

Signed Authored: 2026-08-23 Signed off: 2026-08-23 Replaces: MUTATION_INSTRUCTIONS.html (The Mutation of Facts III, retired to record) Reference: CORE_SYSTEM_DESIGN.html — one system, two documents

Scope: the write machinery — how the game changes its facts. The mutator, the request, the engine and its procedures, the WriteAuthority, the conductor's run, the act and the holster, the presentation pause, and the gather. The entity types, containers, the board, the target resolver, the play road, abilities, the event structure, and conditions are Core System.

§1 The system at a glance

an event fires  →  the cascade gathers the roster  →  the conductor runs each effect  →  each mutator issues  →  the MutationEngine dispatches  →  procedures resolve  →  the WriteAuthority commits  →  events return to the holster  →  the boundary fires them depth-first

One effect firing, end to end:

  1. An event fires. The cascade gathers every eligible effect into the roster (§12) and runs it in order, one effect at a time, each through the EffectConductor.
  2. The conductor engages the effect's target resolution (Core §4), cues the windup, pauses the presenter, and delivers the effect's payload — the unique mutators once, then every mutator per recipient — handing each issuance the plate of facts.
  3. Each mutator reasons over its plate and submits zero or more EngineRequests to the MutationEngine.
  4. The engine hands each request to the procedure that claims its shape. The procedure resolves it into writes committed through the WriteAuthority, issues its presentation cues at commit (§10), and returns the mutation's events. Submission is synchronous.
  5. Events travel back by return into the delivery's holster. The conductor unpauses the presenter at the contact cue — the held cues play — cues the conclusion, and returns. The cascade fires the holster's events at the act boundary, depth-first (§9).

§2 Effects are rules

An effect is a rule of the game. The machinery's effects are the base rules; cards, relics, and statuses add rules on top. Every effect enters the flow by one road: its trigger.

  • The Game entity holds the base rules. It holds the machinery's effects and bears the game's stats — the numbers the base rules consume (mana refill, draws per turn, the strike numbers of §7). A rule that tunes the game writes those stats through the road as any stat mutation.
  • Every write originates in an effect's payload delivery. Machinery dispatches no payloads. It fires first-mover events — the turn clock, the player's asks (Core §7) — and its effects react.
  • The audience is everything that exists. The cascade polls every effect holder in the world — deck, hand, and graveyard units included. Every effect of a unit carries the implied default condition the holder is fielded (housed in a slot's slotted_unit, Core §2), which an authored form may explicitly remove; a non-fielded unit's effects fire only when it does. The condition binds units; holders that are not units are untouched by it. (The removal's authored syntax is out of frame — §14.)

§3 The payload

The payload is a bare typed list on the effect — a bundle of mutators delivered together:

var payload: Array[Mutator]

Parse validation belongs to the effect's parser, delivery belongs to the conductor. One delivery of a payload is the unit of issuance and the scope of the holster (§9).

The authored form is a "payload" array on the effect. Each entry names one mutator: a string where the mutator takes no authored parameters ("strike"), an object naming the mutator with its typed parameters where it does ({"stat_mutation": {"stat": "speed", "delta": 3}}). The parser refuses strangers.

§4 The mutator

A mutator turns an intent — authored by content or constructed by machinery — into concrete requests, using the facts of the firing. A mutator is the only thing that issues requests. One ask is one request, one mutation, one provenance — the mutator's kind.

  • The kind. Every mutator carries a kind: a StringName, its name in the roster below. An authored mutator's kind is its authored name (§3); machinery-only mutators are named the same way, their names just never appear in markup. The kind is the provenance: everything that carries provenance carries this string, and the mutator object itself never travels.
  • Internals are unbounded code. A mutator may read anything and reason however it likes before speaking. The strike mutator asks that the recipient be struck; the draw mutator reads the deck's top from the world and asks that the card move to hand. Markup stays flat, and a new capability is a new mutator in code, never new authoring vocabulary.
  • Contract: issue(plate, recipient) → events. Invoked by the conductor; submits zero, one, or several requests and returns the events those mutations produced.
  • Mutator-to-procedure is many-to-few. Differently-reasoning mutators may all conclude in the same request type.
  • The unique quality. A kind may declare itself unique (Core §4): the conductor runs it once per delivery, before the per-recipient walk, with a null recipient; the mutator appoints its own target from the plate. The conductor reads only the declaration.
  • Destination addressing. A move op elects the destination entity as its target; the mutator introduces the cargo into the MoveRequest (Core §2). Where the entity being moved is the target, the request is routed to its specific-purpose procedure — DrawProcedure, BuryProcedure (§7).
  • Lifecycle: stateless immutable instances. A mutator is constructed once — at parse for an authored effect, in code for a machinery effect — and holds only its authored parameters; every situational fact arrives on the plate. One instance is shared across card copies and simulated worlds. Anything that needs memory is a world fact.

§5 The plate

Because mutator internals are unbounded, mutator inputs cannot be enumerated. Instead, every issuance receives the same closed set of facts. The plate carries two:

  • The occasion — the event that fired the effect.
  • The holder — who holds the firing effect. The world is reached through the holder's world back-reference (Core §1), and every question about game state is answerable through it — which is what lets the plate stay closed.

The recipient — whom this issuance lands on — is a separate typed input beside the plate, supplied by the conductor's walk; a unique mutator's recipient is null (§4).

§6 The EngineRequest

What a mutator issues, and what the engine receives. The facts changing is the mutation; this object is the asking — minting a request is the act of asking, and only mutators mint. It is a dumb data record: a base class carrying the context bundle common to every request, and one subclass per procedure, named by the happening it asks for, carrying that procedure's parameters as typed members.

class_name EngineRequest          # the base: the context bundle
    var mutator_kind: StringName  # the manner of the asking — the kind IS the provenance
    var source: GameEntity        # the holder it came from
    var target: GameEntity        # the target carried from context,
                                  # until the mutator appoints a new one (A16)

class StatMutationRequest extends EngineRequest:
    var stat: StringName
    var delta: int

class StrikeRequest extends EngineRequest:
    pass                          # the striker is the source; the strike procedure
                                  #   reads its attack (§7)

class DamageRequest extends EngineRequest:
    var amount: int
  • The subclass type is the verb. Routing is the type; parameters are typed members.
  • The parameter contract has two parties: the mutators that speak the verb fill the members; the procedure that claims it reads them. The engine transports without reading.
  • A request is complete at construction. Context is stamped at issuance and travels unmodified; a null context slot states that the party genuinely does not exist — never "unknown."
  • The context bundle is a floor. A request may carry more than the three slots; the extension mechanism is future work (§14).

§7 The MutationEngine and its procedures

The MutationEngine is the single entry point for every mutation in the game. The doorway is MutationEngine.submit(request) — static; synchronous, returning the mutation's events. Requests are self-sufficient, so one doorway serves the live fight and every simulated world. The doorway refuses loudly: a request no procedure claims, or a null target where the verb requires one, is an error at submission — never a silent drop — with the type system closing what it can before runtime. Only mutators mint requests, and only the conductor engages mutators, so no submitter exists outside an open delivery.

A procedure is the game's way of resolving one kind of happening into writes and events. A procedure is a fixed part of the engine, stateless — all state lives in the world's facts — and owns one request shape. Its signature is its typed parameters plus the originating request as context; its events are stamped from that context. The stamp is the request itself: every event originated from a mutation request path stamps the request at hand as a RequestEventData. In code, the base class is EngineProcedure; each concrete procedure is named by the happening it resolves, and its request pairs with it by that name.

  • The engine has its procedures fixed in code, as a class has its parts. Its one decision is handing each request to the procedure that claims its shape; every other decision is made before submission, by a mutator, or during resolution, by a procedure. A new happening enters as one new procedure and its request; nothing existing changes.
  • A procedure has two entrances, one body. Asked: a mutator issues the procedure's request; the procedure unpacks it and runs. Applied: a procedure, mid-resolution, runs another procedure directly, passing the parameters it computed and the request already in its hand. Both entrances run the same body, produce the same events, stamp the same way.
  • Application stays inside the one mutation. No request is minted on the applied path. The applying procedure's request travels down as context, so provenance stays singular: a burn's damage carries the burn kind, a strike's damage carries the strike kind, and the damaged event is produced identically on either path.
  • A procedure applies a procedure only when the applied happening happens. Damage happens inside a connecting strike, so the strike procedure applies the damage procedure. Where only a raw write happens, a procedure uses a WriteAuthority primitive (§8).
  • Every decision lives in a procedure; a primitive never decides. A procedure may hold much law, little, or none.

The roster covers every write the game performs today; a happening it does not yet speak enters by the rule above — one new procedure and its request.

The strike mechanics

The chance formula, shared by dodge and crit: chance = base + own Speed × speed rating + difference rating × max(0, Speed difference), capped. The difference term is one-sided: only a positive difference contributes. Dodge rolls from the defender (difference = defender minus attacker); crit rolls from the attacker (difference = attacker minus defender). Rolls draw from the world's seeded rng.

Dodge rolls first. A dodge ends the strike — no damage, no crit — and produces the dodged event. Buildings never dodge.

Crit rolls only on a connecting strike whose damage, after mitigation, is above zero. On success the damage is multiplied by the crit multiplier (flat, capped) and the crit event is produced. Buildings crit and are crit normally.

The pipeline, fixed: dodge → (interception — out of frame, slot reserved) → crit → damage.

The numbers are stats of the Game entity — per mechanic: base, speed rating, difference rating, cap; plus the crit multiplier and its cap. Seeded from data at construction, mutable through the road like any stat. The seeds: dodge — 0% base, 1% per Speed, 4% per difference point, 75% cap; crit — 5% base, 1% per Speed, 0% per difference point, 75% cap, multiplier ×2.0, multiplier cap ×5.0.

§8 The WriteAuthority

Every change to a game fact goes through the WriteAuthority. No bypass, from any origin. It performs writes and decides nothing. It is static, matching the engine: requests are self-sufficient and all state lives in the world's facts, so one authority serves the live fight and every simulated world.

  • Its primitives are exactly four — the stat write, container insert, container remove, and mint — each existing exactly once. The four are an ontology of world structure: a number (the stat write), a membership (insert, remove), an existence (mint). The roster grows only when a procedure needs a decision-free write no primitive covers; per-container law stays above the primitives, in procedures. Primitive signatures are pinned when procedures are written against them. The membership primitives are the only writers of container members, and they maintain the housed entity's back-reference on every write (Core §2).
  • It owns each fact's arithmetic — floors, caps, whatever governs a specific fact's committed value — in exactly one place. Arithmetic bounds the write's performance; it is not a decision.
  • It may produce events on the writes it performs — health reaching zero produces died. A fact event exists only where content cares: none is issued ahead of need, and any fact mutation, crossing or plain change, bears one the moment something cares about it.
  • The authority is the hardening point. Emerging consequences of facts are written into it with relative freedom; it is monolithic at this scope, and its internal structure is deferred until its contents demand one.

§9 The act and the holster

An act is one payload delivery. Within one act, everything commits as it happens; between acts, the world responds. The election settles at engagement (§11) and delivers whole. Every delivery lands whole on every resolved recipient, whatever facts earlier deliveries committed. Mid-walk reads see earlier commits — that is what the delivery order (§11) fixes.

No event is dispatched at its own commit. Events wait in the holster — the payload's container of pending events — and fire together once the payload's last mutation has resolved. One holster per payload delivery, owned by the cascade: the cascade opens the delivery and creates the holster; after the conductor returns, the cascade fires the holster's events; the holster dies when its last event has fired and its consequences have unfolded. Nothing persists between deliveries.

The holster is a queue. Events fire in the order they arrived, and every link of the return chain appends its events in resolution order. Reaction order across a payload follows from the authored payload sequence, the target resolution, and each procedure's body.

Firing is depth-first. An event may trigger a fresh effect, which resolves in full — its own payload, its own holster — before the next event of the outer holster fires.

A death event is an ordinary holstered event, fired and reacted to like any other. Burial is the holder's built-in effect reacting to its own died (Core §6), resolving depth-first at the boundary as any reaction.

§10 Presentation: the pause

A procedure issues its cue on the spot, at commit — named visual, recipient, magnitude — fire-and-forget, synchronous, never awaited. The engine never waits; the return chain carries events alone.

  • The cue's destination is the world's presentation outlet. The live world bears the presenter; a simulated world bears a deaf outlet. A procedure reaches the outlet through the world it writes. Nested and cross-world deliveries cannot collide, and resolution is synchronous (§7), so a paused span is never interleaved within its world.
  • The conductor pauses the presenter before engaging the payload and unpauses at the contact cue (§11). Between the last mutation and the unpause, the presenter holds the full cue set and reshapes it at will — merging, sequencing, pacing: its standing authority.
  • Reception is unconditional; playback is gated. A cue arriving while the presenter is paused or mid-cue queues, in arrival order.
  • Any VFX determines the span of its block before greenlighting the flow.

§11 The EffectConductor

The owner of what running one effect means. The triad's parts each answer one question — when, whom, what — and hold no flow; the conductor asks them in order and tells presentation between the steps. It is built by the cascade from the world alone; presentation is reached through the world's outlet (§10).

The run, exactly:

  1. Engage the effect's target resolution (Core §4). Unknowns settle here, in the live flow: rolls draw from the world's seeded rng; the picker picks. An empty election ends the delivery (Core §4); for an activated ask, an empty election refuses the ask before payment.
  2. Cue the windup (the effect's windup presentation name, the recipients).
  3. Pause the presenter (§10). Deliver: first the payload's unique mutators, once each, recipient null (§4); then per-mutator-then-per-recipient — the payload list is the authored sequence, so its order dominates: each mutator speaks its full ask across all recipients before the next mutator begins. Requests flow to the engine as they are issued.
  4. Cue the contact (the effect's contact presentation name, the recipients). The presenter unpauses here; the held cues play (§10).
  5. Cue the conclusion (the windup name closes).

Every cue is awaited. The flow proceeds when presentation greenlights it, and any VFX determines the span of its block (§10). Blocking lives here, in the flow layer — never in the engine.

The conductor ends at step 5. After it returns, the cascade fires the payload's holster (§9).

§12 The gather and the roster

The gather is a named step of the cascade: an event fires, the cascade gathers every eligible effect into the roster, orders it, and runs it — reactions resolve in roster order. Eligibility is existence: a holder is polled while it exists in the world (§2); the gather inspects no other fact of it — any finer gating is a condition.

Ordering is a two-tier lexicographic comparator, in one place, at the gather: a holder's bucket decides first, the bucket's key decides within. The buckets and their keys:

  1. The Game — a single holder, no key.
  2. The Sides — player before enemy.
  3. Relics — acquisition order.
  4. Cards in hand — hand position.
  5. Fielded units and their statuses — turn order; a status resolves immediately after its holding unit.
  6. Board slots — slot order (per-side).
  7. Graveyard units — order of death.

One principle closes every side tie across the comparator: the player's holders order before the enemy's. It applies in every bucket whose key orders each side internally — hand position, order of death, and slot order are such keys; turn order interleaves the sides and leaves no tie. Within such a bucket, all player holders precede all enemy holders, each side then ordered by its own key.

The table grows by amendment — a new holder kind is a new bucket at a weight, finer granularity is a refined key — and the mechanism never restructures.

§13 Genesis is construction, not mutation

The world is constructed whole: setup builds every entity in its starting container directly, before the first event fires. The write road governs play — every change from the first first-mover event onward. The mint primitive serves existence that begins mid-play; a status is minted and inserted by its procedure (§7).

§14 Out of frame

Named so absence is never read as oversight:

  • Interception — the strike pipeline reserves its slot (§7).
  • The context-bundle extension mechanism (§6).
  • The authored syntax that removes the implied fielded condition (§2) — the payload markup (§3) does not carry it.
  • The procedures' build order.
  • Status removal — expiry and cleanse. Its committed write is a container remove (§8); its procedure enters with the first content that removes a status.
  • Upgrades — de-scoped entirely; they hold no bucket and no place in the entity hierarchy.
  • Spawn — mid-play unit creation; its mutator, request, and procedure enter with the first content that spawns.
