Intent (draft)

The game's entity types carry embedded behaviors — effects the machinery bakes into an entity at construction rather than content authoring: a card's play, a unit's main action, its burial, its Move appointment, a spell's burial, the Game's base rules. All of them stand on the one effect substrate, but each is appointed by its own hand-rolled construction pattern, and no document states, per type, which behaviors the type bears.

This document's purpose: govern the embedded behaviors of entities under one system. When developed, it states per entity type the behaviors machinery embeds, and appoints the one mechanism by which an embedded behavior is declared, built, shared, and conditionally granted — replacing the scattered per-class construction patterns with an appointed logistics. A9's landing depends on it: the Move appointment consolidates into this system.

Entity anatomy

Authored 2026-08-31, replacing the carried Core quotes. The audit's relocations: occupancy, the board-position read, and the Game's plain-member housing land in the types section; the world definition, the ownership topology, and the legal-target ruling stand in the follow-up inventory; Core §2's move-stamping and mover-addressing paragraphs are write-system scope and stay out of this document.

An entity is a bearer of facts and rules. Every entity extends the one root, GameEntity, and bears the members stated here: stats, containers, allegiance, effects, and the world back-reference. The concrete types are instances of this anatomy; what each type declares is the next section's statement.

Stats

A stat is a numeric fact of its bearer. Its identity is its id, a StringName. An entity holds its stats in one map, id → value; a value is a float. Each entity type declares its stat ids in two lists — public mutable, and read-only — and an entity bears exactly its type's declared ids from construction. Reads and writes are generic, by id.

Validation runs twice: parse refuses an id the authored subject type does not declare; execution refuses at the concrete bearer — an unborne id, or a write to a read-only stat. A stat's value changes only through the write road (Mutation §8).

Containers and housing

A container is an ordered holding of entities. One Container class serves every use; its members: owner (GameEntity), name (StringName), and members (ordered Array[GameEntity]). It accepts any GameEntity; membership legality is enforced by the procedures that write it.

An entity holds its containers in one map, name → Container — the same model as stats: identified by StringName, declared per type, accessed generically. The two reads: fetch by name and enumerate. Each entity type declares the container names it bears; GameEntity declares contained, so every entity bears it. Parse refuses a container name the authored subject type does not declare. The container's name member duplicates its map key; the membership primitives maintain both.

Housing. At any moment an entity is a member of exactly one container, or of none. Every housed entity holds a back-reference to the container housing it. The membership primitives — insert and remove (Mutation §8) — are the only writers of container members, and they maintain the back-reference on every write. A move is one procedure performing remove-from-housing then insert-at-destination.

Allegiance

Every entity bears a member referencing the Side it belongs to, stamped at construction as a birth fact and never rewritten. Allegiance is identity, not position: housing may place an entity anywhere; whose it is does not move with it. The read "the holder's side" returns this member.

Effects

Every entity holds effects — the rules it adds to the game. The effect itself — trigger, targeting, payload, lifecycle — is stated in its own section. That section enters at migration pass 2.

The world relation

Every entity bears a back-reference to the world it lives in; the world's definition is Core §1's. Simulation copies the world whole, and a copy carries every entity: each entity reference — both ends of every housing relation, the allegiance, the world back-reference — is remapped to the copy under the established weak-reference discipline.

The entity types

Extending GameEntity directly: Game, Side, Relic, Status, Slot, and Card. Card has two kinds: Unit and Spell, each extending Card.

Each type embeds fixed machinery; content authors only the fields those effects declare authored (Core §5, §6).

  • GameEntity — the base of every entity: stats, containers, housing, allegiance, effects, abilities (command capability is derived by question — Activation).
  • Game — the one rule-bearing entity; houses the two Sides; bears the base rules and their stats.
  • Side — a fighting party; declares deck, hand, graveyard, board, relics; mana is a stat on it; its allegiance is itself.
  • Slot — one board cell; the ground layer's entity; declares slotted_unit. A Slot's coordinate — row and column within its half — is set at construction and never changes. A slot's side is the side owning the board container that houses it (Core §3).
  • Card — the base of the two playable kinds; bears the play effect and the payability query (payability is the play behavior's activatable-now answer — Activation).
  • Unit — a Card kind; bears the main action, the target poll, its burial, the placement baked into its play, and the Move appointment (buildings excepted).
  • Spell — a Card kind; bears its own burial, spent by its play.
  • Status — a condition entity housed in its holder's containment; identity status_id, magnitude stacks.
  • Relic — a side-held effect holder, housed in the side's relics container.

Behaviors — named effects

A behavior is an effect defined once, under a name, in the behavior registry. The registry's home is unappointed — data file, document seat, and loading road await ruling. A card obtains a behavior by its name. Machinery embedding and content reuse are one mechanism: an entity type's embedded behaviors are named behaviors appended at construction, and a card definition appends the same way.

Definition. A behavior's definition is the effect's authored markup with slot tags standing where inputs land. Each slot tag carries its default value; a bare invocation is complete, and every supplied input is an override. The named slots are the behavior's input contract. An input is markup, spliced at its slot's position by the expansion; the expanded effect parses as any effect.

The slot tag is an object. It stands at a value position of the markup and bears the tag marker; the expansion recognizes a slot by the marker alone. The slot's name is read only by the fill lookup — its own namespace — so a name shared with a markup property key collides with nothing: keys are found at key positions, slots by their marker at value positions. Working syntax, marker spelled @: {"@": "targeting", "default": ...} — awaiting minting.

Invocation. The effects array of a definition holds either a fully authored effect, or an invocation: an object bearing the behavior key naming the definition, every other key an input.

{"behavior": "bury"}
{"behavior": "strike", "decision": "nearest"}
{"behavior": "play", "targeting": {...}, "payload": [...]}

Nesting. A slot's value — supplied or defaulted — may itself be an invocation. The baker expands invocations until the markup is plain effect text, then parses it. A name revisited within one expansion is refused loudly.

The bake. Appending bakes a copy of the expanded definition at that moment. Entities build fresh from content at each fight's genesis, so a changed definition reaches every subsequent build. Baked-copy over live-linking is ruled: the benefit of linking is narrow — behaviors changing at runtime are unexpected — and linking stays available as a later initiative.

Ownership. Every entity owns its baked instance. The instance is immutable and holds only its definition; the plate supplies the holder per consultation, and world copies carry the instance untouched. Per-entity ownership over a sharing cache is ruled: sharing buys memory nobody needs against real keying machinery, and it is what made reference identity ambiguous. Owner-blindness is kept for the world copies: an owning back-reference would force effect deep-copies and remaps at every preview declaration.

Abilities. The shared ability functionality is held in a behavior; a concrete ability is that behavior extended through its inputs.

Generality. Any effect can be defined as a behavior and shared among cards by name plus inputs — parameterization is open to all content, beyond the machinery-appended set.

Activation — the ActivatedTrigger

An effect is triggered on its holder's command through a second trigger kind: the ActivatedTrigger. Appointing a behavior's trigger kind as activated grants the command capability in the same act as appending the behavior. The firer is the effect's holder.

The event, outbound. The ActivatedTrigger holds an appointed event — the one it fires. On command it mints the ask event, carrying itself by reference in a new named EventData type minted for this purpose alone (working label: ActivationEventData). Equality is reference identity; one trigger per effect and one instance per entity make the reference single out exactly one consultee in the world.

The fired event drives the payment flow. The ask event exists because the commanded road needs it: the payment request is issued, the affordability conditions validate, a valid target is picked, and then the substantive effect proceeds — all controlled by the existing event flow.

Engagement. The ask fires through the cascade like any event: the walk builds the plate at the holder, and bystanders react to the event's name. The ActivatedTrigger's proc test is one condition — the event carries me — standing where the listening trigger's event-name comparison stands. Its further conditions validate the state of the world, affordability among them. Carrying the reference and carrying an id produce the same mechanism; the reference is chosen because effects hold no identifier scheme, and equality is reference identity everywhere — the ruled position for entities, followed. Pending ratification: for bystanders discriminating which behavior fired, the behavior's name rides the ask and engaged events as event data.

The command questions. Two questions serve command, posed by the entity walking its own effects:

  • Do you activate? — a fixed fact of the effect, deciding whether a command surface exists for it: the abilities tray builds from the yeses.
  • Are you activatable now? — a live, side-effect-free read of the world, deciding whether that surface is interactable at this time.

Effects are sealed: the effect answers both questions itself, casting its own trigger; the answer alone crosses the seal.

Fire. The commander addresses the effect — activatable-now, then fire — and the effect mints its own ask. The event name stays inside the effect.

The whole mechanism. The ActivatedTrigger is all of it: the two questions, the firing, and the carrying-me condition.

Peripheral, unaddressed: play and Move special-cased as autocast — commanded without surfacing in the abilities tray — with ability autocast built on the same rails. The main action stays a listening trigger: act is fired at the unit by the clock, not commanded by the holder.

Move's appointment (ruling, transferred from A9)

Moving costs nothing in this version. The use_ability ask for Move is fired by the moving UI already in place; that UI is adapted as needed to obtain the target slots and fire the event. (Transferred from A3 on Enrra's word, 2026-08-27.) Machinery appoints the Move ability on every unit at construction — conditionally: buildings do not receive it. Ruled by Enrra, 2026-08-23.
