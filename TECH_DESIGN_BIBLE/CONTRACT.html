§1 Goals

  • Short term: a vertical slice of all systems.
  • Long term: functional parity with the pre-nuke version.

§2 The rulings

  1. The tech design is legally binding, and must be respected with precision, to full extent.
  2. Found work is documented for ruling. Work the documentation does not cover is surfaced and recorded the moment it is found; it proceeds only under a ruling.
  3. Self-unblocking is an exceptional resource. Where the work is blocked on a decision the documentation does not rule, the contractor may decide and proceed. Each such decision carries a contractual obligation: it is recorded in a brief at the moment it is made and submitted for validation. Until validated the decision is provisional; validation confirms it into ruling text, and rejection renders the work standing on it non-conforming, to be corrected. A decision that can wait for ruling, waits.
  4. Legacy code is not a reference. No legacy code is treated as a valid reference. Code that aligns perfectly to the document stays; otherwise it is cleansed entirely. Any piece of leftover code that collides with the document is a legal liability.

§3 The plan

The build order, the slice's definition, and its coverage: IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.html.

§4 Approach

Contractor's insight, offered — not ruling text.

The slice comes first because it is the cheapest complete proof: every designed system executes at least once, on minimal content, before any content scale is attempted — a flaw in the ground surfaces while the ground is small. Parity is then reached by widening, not by new mechanism: content migrates into the signed envelope, UI features reconnect progressively under A10, and every engineering demand content makes enters through the Content Dictionary's gate and lands as a ruled amendment. The rulings of §2 hold at every step of both goals.
