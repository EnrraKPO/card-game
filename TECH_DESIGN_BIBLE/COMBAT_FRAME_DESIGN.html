Combat Frame Design

Signed Authored: 2026-08-23 Signed off: 2026-08-23 Absorbs: AMENDMENTS.html A2, A5, A6 as primary text Reference: Core System Design · Mutation System Design

Scope: the frame that drives one fight — the clock, the round and its spans, the base rules the clock's events engage, the activation order, the tap fact, and the fight's ending. Everything the frame's events reach runs on the card system and the write system.

§1 The fight

A fight is a sequence of rounds. Each side fields a king: a unit bearing the king birth fact, authored in the card envelope (A7). A king's death ends the fight: the enemy king's death is victory, the player king's death is defeat. When one act kills both kings, the fight is a defeat.

§2 The clock

The clock is machinery embedded in the world, the frame's only moving part. It fires the frame's first-mover events (Mutation §2) and advances the round through its spans; every change of fact it causes is performed by effects reacting through the road. The clock decides two things alone: which event fires next, and when the fight has ended (§7).

§3 The round

One round, in order:

  1. The opening — the clock fires round_started; the Game's base rules run (§4).
  2. The enemy's command span — the enemy commands (§5).
  3. The player's command span — the player commands (§5).
  4. The combat span — every fielded unit's moment, in activation order (§6).

The next round opens when the combat span's last moment has resolved. The fight's ending interrupts the round wherever it lands (§7).

§4 The opening and the base rules

round_started is a fact event, source the Game, fired by the clock at each round's open. The Game's base rules trigger on it and run in their declared order:

  1. The round count rule — raises the Game's round stat by one.
  2. The untap rule — clears every fielded unit's tapped stat (§6).
  3. The ramp rule — raises each side's mana_capacity by the ramp amount. Capacity is uncapped.
  4. The refill rule — raises each side's mana to its capacity.
  5. The draw rule — each side draws the turn draw from its deck.

Each rule is an ordinary effect: trigger on round_started, resolver electing its recipients, payload of roster mutators. The numbers are Game stats, seeded from data and mutable through the road (Mutation §2):

§5 The command spans

The enemy commands first, then the player (A5). Within its span, a commander fires the ask events the card system defines — play, use_ability, Move's ask among the latter — at will, each ask resolving in full before the next.

  • The player's span is served by the interaction layer: the existing hand, board, and moving UI fire the asks; the span ends when the player ends the turn.
  • The enemy's span is served by the enemy commander — the encounter engine behind the same ask vocabulary; the span ends when the commander yields.

A unit fielded during a command span acts in that same round's combat span (A5).

§6 The combat span

The activation order

At the span's open the clock computes the activation order once — every fielded unit, ordered by the activation comparator: speed descending; ties to the player's units; ties then to forward depth (the unit furthest forward on its own half first); ties then to row order. Acceptance standard: the comparator reproduces the current game's ordering. The order is computed once per round and is the single definition both the clock and the turn-order display walk.

The moment

The clock walks the order and fires the ask event act at each unit still fielded at its moment; a unit no longer fielded is passed over. The ask is fired at every fielded unit, tapped or not — bystanders and the unit's statuses react to the moment through their own triggers, in gather order (Mutation §12).

The main action

The main action is fixed Unit machinery (Core §1) with the standard effect structure:

  • Trigger: event act; the route-source + is_holder entry; the baked condition that the holder is untapped.
  • Targeting: the attack resolver — conditions narrowing to enemy units, the decision ranking by BoardGeometry's attack-preference comparator (Core §3), electing one.
  • Payload: the tap mutator — machinery only, unique (Core §4): recipient null, it appoints its holder and concludes in a StatMutationRequest setting tapped — then the strike mutator.

Acting is free: the ask carries no pay mutator and no engaged pair — the strike procedure's events are the happening record. The target poll is this resolver's resolve phase, run at interactive idle for the previews (Core §4).

The tap fact

tapped is a public mutable unit stat: zero is untapped, above zero is tapped; its arithmetic floors at zero (Mutation §8). It is spent by the main action's tap mutator and by tap-priced ability costs (PayCostProcedure, Mutation §7), and cleared by the untap rule at the round's opening (§4). The tap mutator's row joins the mutator roster (Mutation §4) by this document.

§7 The fight's ending

The clock ends the fight when a king's died event has fired and the delivery it belongs to has unfolded whole — the holster drained, every reaction resolved (Mutation §9). The ended fight fires no further first-mover events; the frame reports victory or defeat (§1) to the run layer through the existing combat-end seam.

§8 Genesis

Setup constructs the fight whole (Mutation §13): both sides' decks shuffled by the world's seeded rng, each opening hand of the seeded size built directly into its hand container, each side's mana_capacity and mana seeded to the starting capacity, kings and any authored starting units housed on their slots. The first round_started is the first event ever fired.

§10 Out of frame

Named so absence is never read as oversight:

  • Surrender — the captain's yield exists in the current game; it re-enters at the parity pass.
  • Round limits and timers.
  • Extra or repeated spans within a round.
  • Mid-combat-span arrivals — spawn is out of frame whole (Mutation §14).
