The Anatomy of Cards

Rejected — superseded Elaborated: 2026-08-23 Rejected: 2026-08-23 (Enrra) — unprofessional redaction; superseded by CORE_SYSTEM_DESIGN.html Draft record: CARD_INSTRUCTIONS_DRAFT.html Reference: MUTATION_INSTRUCTIONS.html (The Mutation of Facts III) — one system, two documents; §10 lists the amendments it receives

The core of playable things: what a card, a spell, and a unit are, and how triggered and activated effects give them behavior. This document defines those kinds, the board they stand on, and the full road from a player's ask to a delivered play.

§1 Charter

This document settles:

  • The kinds — card, spell, unit: what each is, where each lives, which capacities each bears, and the baked reactions each kind carries.
  • The board — the slots, their coordinates, the sides' halves, the BoardManager, and the geometry functions.
  • The two effect natures — triggered and activated: how each attaches to its holder and how each comes to run.
  • The play road — the carrier and the road for everything a play needs beyond the source's identity: the appointed targets, the destination slot.
  • The ability — an entity's capability to fire an event at will, and the bundle that dresses it.
  • The event anatomy — the shape of a happening: the core facts every event bears and the components it accretes on the road.
  • The condition — the predicate serving in effects and resolvers: its kinds, its authored form, its subjects and their routing.

§2 The entity anatomy

The kind chain spends inheritance on card-ness. Card derives from GameEntity and embeds the machinery every playable thing shares: the play effect (§5) and the payability query. Unit derives from Card and embeds the machinery every unit shares: the main action, the target poll, the placement reactor on play_engaged, and the burial reactor on died (§6). Spell derives from Card beside Unit and embeds the spell's machinery: the burial reactor on play_engaged (§6). A kind with embedded machinery is what a type expresses; the type is the home of the appointment slots.

Slot is a GameEntity. Its coordinate — row and column within its half — is an immutable birth fact, the slot's identity on the board. The coordinate carries no side component: the slot's side is the housing read — my housing's owner (§3). The slot bears a named container, slotted unit, and board occupancy is membership in it: a unit housed in a slot's slotted-unit container is fielded there. Placement legality lives where every invariant lives — in the membership primitives all writes pass through.

The container is an object, one class for every use. Its identity: the owner entity and a name; its substance: ordered Array[GameEntity] members. It admits any GameEntity; membership legality is the writing procedure's law. Any entity organizes its containment into named containers as its anatomy warrants — a side names deck, hand, graveyard, and board (§3); a slot names slotted unit.

Every housed entity bears a back-reference to its container object. The owner is one hop away through the container's owner member; the entity thereby knows both its holder and which of the holder's containers houses it. The membership primitives — the only writers of members — maintain the back-reference with every insert and remove. Fielded reads as: my housing is a slot's slotted-unit container. A unit's board position reads as: my housing's owner's coordinate.

The one-housing law: an entity is housed in exactly one container object, or unhoused at mint. A move is remove-from-housing plus insert-at-destination, one procedure's act.

The move procedure stamps its happening. A container move stamps the origin housing and the destination as EventData on the event it produces; a condition gates on the happening's origin read from the component, and the mover's current housing stays a separate, world-side read.

The arrival has one author. The membership procedure, on any insert into a slotted-unit container, stamps the origin and destination housings and produces the fact event fielded, source the arriving unit. Every road onto the board — play or summon — produces the arrival by construction, and on-arrival triggers gate on fielded without knowing which road delivered them.

The mover mutator's destination address. A container is addressed by owner plus name. A mover mutator's recipient is the destination's owner, and the destination container's name is a fact of the mutator — baked into the kind where the kind has one office (placement names slotted unit, burial names graveyard), a declared typed member where a general container-move kind is authored. A container is never a recipient; recipients stay entities, and the name completes the address.

Occupancy is a fact of content. A slot's slotted-unit container holds what play puts there; current content fields one unit per slot, and the mechanics carry any count.

Any GameEntity can be a target. Target eligibility spans the entity root; narrowing is the work of each resolver's authored conditions.

Geometry is a read layer. Distance, area, adjacency, and row-and-column reasoning compute from slot coordinates and occupancy, through the same reads as everything else (§3).

Implementation note: back-references follow the established weak-reference discipline, and world copies remap both ends of every housing relation.

§3 The board and the geometry

Each side owns a container named board, housing its half's slots. A slot's side is thereby a housing fact, read as my housing's owner — never a stored component of the coordinate.

BoardManager — one instance, embedded in the world. It owns behavior, no content; the container stays the one class (§2), and the board machinery is a class beside it, never an extension. Its duties:

  • The birth clause. At the world's birth it mints both halves' slots, stamps their coordinates, houses them through the membership primitives, and files each in the index in the same act.
  • The fetch. Address → slot: a direct map written once at birth over immutable facts, never invalidated and never rebuilt. A slot fetch is one lookup, never a search. The manager also offers the read "slots at these addresses".

The manager computes no shape and owns no comparator.

The geometry functions are a pure class of static functions (name pending ruling): stateless, world-blind; coordinates and the halves' facing relation in, coordinates out. Its members:

  • The symmetric distance — two coordinates in, no notion of facing.
  • The attack-preference comparator — the attack targeting ordering, fed the facing as an input. Its standard is behavioral: the current orderings are the expected ones.
  • The AOE shapes — cone, row, column, radius, square.

The mirroring arithmetic between the two halves lives here alone; no slot and no side reasons about it.

The composer is the resolver (§4). Conditions narrow by entity facts; the decision ranks by a named geometry function — nearest by the distance, the attack decision by the preference comparator. The composition is one motion: geometry yields addresses, the manager's fetch yields slots, occupancy reads onward through the slot's container.

Allegiance and standing are two reads. A unit's allegiance is its birth fact; the half it stands on is read through its slot's housing — the side owning the board container. The reads answer truthfully when they differ.

§4 The target resolver

The resolver elects the recipients of the payload. That election is its one office in the triad. It is fed the plate — the same facts a mutator receives, because both are unbounded in how they use the information: the occasion and the holder (§9), with each candidate supplied as the typed subject and the world reached through the holder. Through the occasion and the world it can resolve into anything.

Its anatomy is ordered: conditions, then decision. Conditions narrow the world to eligible candidates; the decision picks among the survivors. The decision mechanisms are a family — nearest, random, stat-ranked, hand-pick, the occasion's targets — and hand-picking is one mechanism among them: a manual resolver is a resolver whose decision consults the player.

The election runs in two phases. Resolve is the side-effect-free phase: conditions applied, the field narrowed as far as determinism reaches. It commits nothing, so any reader may run it at any moment — the interactive-idle target poll, the preview world, the greyout all read resolve. Engage is the committing phase: unknowns settle — the roll rolls, the picker picks — once, in the live flow. Engagement is issued uniformly for every resolver, and engaging is the hinge that fires the escalation into payload delivery; a resolver with nothing unknown answers its engagement from its resolution. Rolls made at engagement draw from the world's seeded rng.

An engagement can be declined. A declined pick yields an empty election: the effect is targetless, it does not trigger, and the payload never runs. For an activated ask this ends the ask whole, with everything unpaid.

An effect authored without a resolver targets the Game. The default is a fact of the authored form, decided at birth. An authored resolver is a commitment: it names the shape of its candidates and either elects them or elects nobody — and an empty election ends the delivery.

Events carry their targets as a set. An effect's elected targets travel in the events its delivery produces — stamped as EntityEventData under the role targets, a singular target riding as a set of one (§8) — and downstream resolvers read them from the occasion. Derivations are authored as conditions — "adjacent to the occasion's targets" is a resolver reading the occasion and narrowing by geometry.

The unique mutator. A mutator kind may bear the unique quality: the conductor runs it once per delivery, first, before the per-recipient walk, handing it the plate with a null recipient (§9). A unique mutator appoints its own target internally, from the plate. The quality is declared by the kind; the conductor reads the declaration and the seal stays shut.

§5 The play road

Every card bears the play effect, machinery of the Card type. Content authors its targeting; the effect itself is baked. Its anatomy:

  • Trigger: play; the played card is the event's source (§8).
  • Conditions: affordability — the cost stat within open mana — and the holder being in hand; authored conditions join them.
  • Targeting: authored (resolver and conditions). The defaults are type facts: a card assumes auto/Game; a unit assumes manual/Slot. The engagement is where the player's pick happens; a declined pick ends the play unpaid.
  • Payload: the pay mutator, a unique mutator — it runs once per play, ahead of the walk, and addresses the holder's side, whose mana pays.

Cost is a card stat — seeded from the authored form at construction, mutable through the road like any stat. Discounts and surcharges are ordinary stat mutations.

The payment produces play_engaged, carrying the play's elected targets as its EntityEventData under the role targets (§8). The card's substantive effects trigger on play_engaged under the source route's is_holder condition (§8, §9), and their resolvers read the occasion: the stock form takes the occasion's targets as its own; authored forms derive from them by condition.

§6 The baked reactions of the kinds

The kinds' landings and burials are ordinary baked effects — each an effect of the kind's machinery, built from the same anatomy every effect has, and each reacting to the event that concerns its holder under the source route's is_holder condition (§9).

The unit's placement. An effect of the Unit kind. Trigger play_engaged; gate: entity entry, route source, condition is_holder. Its resolver is the stock form taking the occasion's targets as its own, electing the play's slot (§5). Its payload is the placement mutator: recipient the elected slot, act the container move — the holder leaves its housing and enters the recipient's slotted-unit container, one procedure's act under the one-housing law (§2). The insert produces fielded (§2).

The unit's burial. An effect of the Unit kind. Trigger died; gate: entity entry, route source, condition is_holder. Its payload is the burial mutator: the container move to the graveyard. died is a fact event authored by the WriteAuthority (III §8): one author for the crossing, and every road to zero health produces it by construction.

The spell's burial. An effect of the Spell kind. Trigger play_engaged — the spell is spent by its play; gate: entity entry, route source, condition is_holder. Its payload is the burial mutator.

Burial-reactors are decoupled from depletion-reactors. The move's procedure stamps the origin housing and the destination as EventData (§2), and a condition gates on the happening's origin read from the component; the mover's current housing stays a separate, world-side read.

§7 The ability

An ability is its holder's capability to fire an event at will — machinery any GameEntity can bear. That capability is the whole of the machinery's contribution; everything downstream of the event is authored content. The bundle that dresses the capability into a usable ability is an amenity over it.

Ask events are imperative verbs. The event vocabulary splits by direction: an ask going in is an imperative — play, use_heal — and a happening coming out is past tense — play_engaged, use_heal_engaged, died. The grammar names the side of the road a name sits on.

Every ability is a separate identity, and the identity lives in its event names. An ability named Heal owns the ask use_heal and the engagement use_heal_engaged; both derive from its name. Firing the ask names the ability whole — the ask carries no further cargo for it. The name is the event core's name fact (§8); the ask rides the core alone, componentless.

The bundle. Appointing the ability Heal on an entity expands, at construction, into three pieces:

  • The ask capability: the holder can have use_heal fired at it at will.
  • The use effect, built on the play effect's anatomy: trigger use_heal; conditions — affordability and the authored gating; targeting authored; payload — the pay mutator. Its payment produces use_heal_engaged, carrying the elected targets as its EntityEventData under the role targets (§8).
  • The substantive effect — authored — triggering on use_heal_engaged under the source route's is_holder condition (§8, §9).

The two discriminators bind by construction. The event name identifies the ability's kind; the source route's is_holder condition binds each instance to its own holder's engagement. Two units bearing Heal share the event names and are told apart by the condition. Both bindings are the bundle's work; the author wires nothing.

The engaged name derives from the occasion, uniformly. The plate's occasion holds the event that fired the effect; the pay mutator reads the ask's name there and derives the produced event's name from it — play into play_engaged, use_heal into use_heal_engaged. One derivation serves both roads.

Abilities are authored through a markup form, the only authoring door. The form requests the authored fields alone: the ability's name, its cost, its targeting policy, the substantive effect. The expansion into the pieces — the ask event, the use effect, the name derivations, the discriminating conditions — is programmatic baking at construction, concealed below the form. The triggered-effect ground the mechanism extends stays machinery's vocabulary; the markup requests only what varies. The play road holds the same stance: the play effect is baked, and content authors only its fields (§5).

The play and the use are similar mechanisms, not the same one. The play effect is machinery of the Card type, one per card, asked by play; a use effect is minted per appointed ability, asked by the ability's own name. Both share the one anatomy.

§8 The event anatomy

An event is a core plus components. The core holds the two facts inherent to every happening: the source — the entity the happening comes from, and any GameEntity can be a source — and the event's name. Every further fact rides in the event's component list: Array[EventData].

EventData is a typed class family, one class per shape of fact. Each component carries its fact as typed members — StatMutationEventData the stat and the delta, MutatorEventData the manner of a mutation: the mutator's kind. A fact of an existing shape with a different purpose is not a new class — the container's ruling holds here (§2, one class for every use): purpose is a name. A fact's carrier class is the same on every event that carries the fact; the class family is the vocabulary of event facts.

Entity-bearing facts ride one class: EntityEventData. Its members: the role — a StringName naming what the entities are to the happening, targets among them — and the entities, Array[GameEntity], a singular fact riding as a set of one. The gate's bare event-data route (§9) pools every EntityEventData's yield; narrowed, it narrows by role.

The event accretes its components as it travels. Each layer of the road appends the components it knows: an event passing through a mutator gains its MutatorEventData; a delivery that elected targets stamps them as EntityEventData under the role targets; a procedure stamps the facts of its happening. Appending is open to anything on the road, and each layer's stamp is its own contribution.

Reading is fetch-by-shape. A reader names a component class and receives the matching components, reading their typed members. An event may carry any count of components of one shape; the fetch serves any or all, and the reader governs its use of several.

A condition asking of an absent component evaluates false. Provenance interrogation keeps its two questions over this shape: the source answers who, the MutatorEventData answers how.

The trigger names the event. An effect's trigger names its event, and the naming is itself a condition of the effect; the effect's further conditions read the named occasion's components from there.

The activation road's source. The ask event's source is the activated entity: use_heal fires with the asked unit as its source, play with the played card. The engaged event's source is the same entity, stamped by the procedure from the request. The discriminating condition is is_holder under the source route (§9) — one predicate, both stages, every ability and every play.

§9 The condition

A condition is a predicate. It asks one question of the facts before it and answers true or false. It decides nothing beyond its answer and writes nothing.

The condition serves in two seats. In an effect, conditions gate the firing: the trigger's naming of the event is itself a condition (§8), and the further conditions join it — every one must hold for the effect to run. In a resolver, conditions narrow the world to the eligible candidates before the decision picks (§4). One concept serves both seats.

Conditions read event components by shape. A condition interrogating the occasion names a component class and reads the matching components' typed members (§8). A condition asking of an absent component evaluates false.

The rostered members so far. Two authored kinds are established. is_holder, an EntityCondition with no members: the subject is the plate's holder. At the gate, under the source route, it is the discriminating condition of every play and every ability (§8); the source fetch is the route's work, never the predicate's. has_status, an EntityCondition with one member, status_id: StringName: the subject's contained holds a Status of that id, read by checked downcast (III §9). The machinery's baked conditions — affordability and holder-in-hand on the play effect (§5), affordability on the use effect (§7) — are conditions of those effects' anatomy, not authored content. Membership grows under Open 8.

Derivations are conditions. A targeting rule that derives from the occasion — "adjacent to the occasion's targets" — is a resolver condition reading the occasion's target set and narrowing by geometry (§4).

The working material is the plate plus a typed subject. The plate is two facts: the occasion (an Event) and the holder (a GameEntity). The world is reached through the holder — every entity bears a back-reference to the world it lives in (III). The subject — the object the predicate runs on — is a separate input, typed, supplied by the seat: the resolver hands each candidate; the gate hands EventConditions the occasion and EntityConditions their routed yield (routing below). The same plate serves mutators, whose recipient is likewise a separate typed input.

The family is the subject's type. A condition's base class declares what it tests and thereby its signature: EntityCondition tests a GameEntity, EventCondition tests an Event; concrete kinds derive within their base. A seat supplies one subject type and accepts that family: resolver conditions are EntityConditions; the gate runs both families — EventConditions on the occasion, EntityConditions on routed subjects; the trigger's naming is the EventCondition family's founding member.

The gate routes its entity subjects. An EntityCondition serving at the gate names its subject's origin — the route, authored on the entry beside the conditions. Four routes exist: source — the occasion's core; holder — the plate's; event data — bare, pooling every EntityEventData's yield, or narrowed by role (§8); and world — every entity that exists, reached through the holder's world. The route list is closed: the world is the top scope, and any narrower fetch is the world narrowed by conditions. Every route yields a set of entities: source and holder as sets of one, a named role absent from the occasion as the empty set — genuine absence (§8).

The effect fetches; the condition only answers. Routing exists because an entity subject has a choice of origins, and the choice is authored outside the predicate. The effect owns the fetch: its gate walk reads each entry's route, gathers the yield, and hands each entity to the conditions. EventConditions carry no route — the occasion has one home, the plate — and read their components by shape themselves (§8). The rule: the effect supplies what the condition cannot locate alone.

Two lists, one gate. The effect holds its gating conditions in two typed lists, sorted at parse by family — EventConditions and entity entries. The gate holds when both lists hold; an empty list holds vacuously. Each list bears its own all-or-any policy across its entries.

The entity entry: any-fold under a negate. An entry is one route, a list of conditions conjoined per entity, and the entry's negate. The fold is any alone: the entry asks whether any entity of the yield satisfies every condition, and the negate — a handle over the folded answer — turns the existential claim universal. "All enemies are wounded" is authored "not: any (enemy, healthy)". The per-entity conjunction narrows and tests in one motion; no narrowing role and no all-fold exist at the entry. The base negate (below) keeps its seat — it inverts one condition's answer per entity; the entry's negate inverts the entry's.

The authored form of the gate. An effect's gating block holds the two lists under one key per family; each list bears its policy; an entity entry bears its route, its negate, and its conditions:

"gate": {
  "event":  { "policy": "all", "conditions": [
    { "kind": "mutator_is", "mutator": "poison" }
  ]},
  "entity": { "policy": "all", "entries": [
    { "route": "source", "conditions": [ { "kind": "is_holder" } ] },
    { "route": "world", "negate": true, "conditions": [
      { "kind": "is_enemy" },
      { "kind": "stat_vs_stat", "left": "health", "op": "lt", "right": "max_health", "negate": true }
    ]}
  ]}
}

The route token is source, holder, world, eventdata for the pooled yield, or a role for that role's components (§8). Absent members take their defaults: policy all, negate false, an absent list empty — holding vacuously. The generic driver (below) parses the whole block; kinds shown beyond the roster are illustrative.

The families share the authored skeleton, never the call. One base class carries what every family authors alike — the kind id, the negate handle, the declared typed members and their parse, the stateless-immutable lifecycle — so the driver and the kind table stay single. The evaluation signature lives per family; no shared evaluate exists — a loosely typed subject is exactly the input this design refuses.

The family roster is open by subject type. A subject neither entity nor event founds a new family with its own routes — the container is the standing case: an origin or destination housing is a container, not a GameEntity. Membership grows under Open 8.

The predicate is the class; the handles are its members. Content never authors logic — it selects a kind by its id and fills the kind's declared typed members. A stat comparison is one kind with three members:

{ "kind": "stat_compare", "stat": "strength", "op": "gt", "value": 5 }

A check no existing kind supports is a new class, extending the family its subject dictates, plus one row in the kind table.

The stat identity. A stat's identity is its id, a StringName. Every entity kind declares its stats in two lists — public mutable and read-only. Validation runs at two gates: parse refuses an id absent from the authored subject kind's declaration; execution refuses at the concrete bearer — an unborne id, or a write addressing a read-only stat.

The collection combines; the condition never does. Each seat's condition list bears a policy — all or any — and the combination vocabulary is the policies, the two negates, and the entity entry's any-fold (above); a condition holding conditions never exists.

Negation is a base handle. Every condition bears negate: bool, a member of the base class, false unless authored. The kind's class computes its question in its natural sense; the machinery inverts the answer where the handle is set. The driver parses the handle as base vocabulary beside the kind id. Over an absent component the inverted answer is true — absence is a genuine fact of the happening (§8), so the negated question truthfully holds.

Lifecycle: stateless immutable instances. A condition is constructed at parse, holds only its authored members, and never accumulates state across evaluations; one instance is shared safely across card copies and simulated worlds. Anything that needs memory is a world fact.

Parse is one generic driver. The kind id resolves to the class through a static table; the driver fills the class's declared members by name from the markup, refusing strangers and type mismatches loudly; serialization is the mirror walk. No kind carries parse code; the driver grows only when the value-type vocabulary grows. Authoring a kind is the class and its table row, nothing further.

Text survives parse nowhere. An authored member parses into its real type — a stat's name becomes the stat's identity, or refuses loudly because no such stat exists. The conversion is the validation; no checking layer follows it.

§10 Amendments to The Mutation of Facts III

Each lands on III at the amendment pass; III's statements on spells and abilities are superseded by this document.

  • §10–§11, the hierarchy: board presence relocates from a base type onto Slot; Unit derives from Card; the inheritance chart redraws.
  • §7, the primitive ontology: the membership primitives (insert, remove) carry board occupancy; the ontology's categories become number, membership, and existence.
  • Open 21's container clauses: naming generalizes from the side to any entity; the container grows its object form (owner, name, members) as primary text.
  • §5, the context bundle: the target slot dissolves — elected targets ride the event as EntityEventData under the role targets (§8).
  • §5, the plate: the plate narrows to two facts — occasion and holder; the world member is trimmed (reached through the holder's world back-reference); the recipient leaves the plate and becomes the seat's separate typed input.
  • §4, the mutator lifecycle: the stateless-immutable clause extends to conditions verbatim.
  • §11, the conductor's run: the election splits into resolve and engage; the run opens with the unique mutators; the empty-resolution clause takes §4's vocabulary — for an activated ask the empty election refuses the ask before payment.
  • §15 and Open 27: the pay effect is the play effect — machinery of the Card type, named by its office; the payability clause and the activation language restate over the §5 anatomy.
  • §15–§16, the ask vocabulary: activation_invoked is play; ask events form the imperative family beside the past-tense happenings; the pay mutator's produced event name derives from the occasion — cost_paid is play_engaged.
  • §12, the death clause: the innate burial consequence dissolves — the cascade executes no burial. Burial is an ordinary baked effect of the kind, reacting to its holder's depletion event (§6).
