Build pitch · first phase of the effect-system rebuild

The Attack System

Signed off · 2026-08-11 Authority: TARGETING_DESIGN.md (§13) Date: 2026-08-11 Branch: targeting-cleanup

Combat is currently inert: units take their turns but nothing swings. This initiative makes units fight again — and in doing so, builds and proves every core piece of the new effect machinery on the simplest real behavior in the game: when my turn comes, I hit the nearest enemy.

§1What the player gets

At the end of this initiative, fights work: each unit, at its speed-ordered moment, attacks the enemy its policy chooses; dodge, shield, and crit behave as before; kills pay bounties; and the board shows who is aiming at whom (crosshair on my target, menace glow on whoever targets me). Outwardly this restores what the demolition removed. Inwardly, every step now runs on the new machinery — and that machinery is the same one spells, abilities, statuses, and relics will ride later, which is the real prize.

§2How one attack happens

The whole design in one pass — each numbered step is performed by one named part from §3:

flowchart TD
  A["A unit's turn arrives
act event fires"] --> B["Dispatch asks every effect:
do you react? (TriggerResolver)"]
  B --> C["The unit's referenced melee_attack
named effect fires (a TriggeredEffect)"]
  C --> D["ActionExecutor runs it"]
  D --> E["TargetResolver picks the victim
(NearestResolver: the settled ordering)"]
  E --> F["AttackPayload delivers the strike"]
  F --> G["Mutator derives the amount
(holder's attack stat)"]
  G --> H["Arbitration Resolver commits damage
(dodge · shield · crit, as today)"]
  H --> I["Events emitted as news:
attack · struck · dodge · crit · kill"]
  1. A unit's activation moment arrives. The game emits the act event — an ordinary event in the one pipeline, nothing attack-specific about it.
  2. Dispatch asks every live effect's TriggerResolver (already built): do you fire for this event? The unit's referenced named effect — melee_attack, authored once in the library as "when I act" — says yes.
  3. The ActionExecutor runs the effect: it asks the effect's TargetResolver "what are you pointing at?" and receives the resolution — for nearest, the enemy chosen by the most-playtested rule in the game (column depth dominates, lane offset breaks ties, deterministic address after that).
  4. The executor assembles the feed — the four facts (world, holder, occasion, recipient) — and hands it to the AttackPayload, which asks its Mutator to derive the amount: the holder's attack stat, read fresh at that instant.
  5. The payload proposes the strike to the arbitration Resolver — the single writer — which applies dodge, shield, and crit exactly as it does today, and commits.
  6. The results become events (attack, struck, dodge, crit, kill) — post-hoc news that other effects may react to, continuing the cascade.

§3The cast — every name that will exist in code

Tagged new (built by this initiative) or exists (already in the codebase, used as-is).

TriggeredEffectnew scripts/effects/triggered_effect.gd

The action structure from the settled design: when [trigger], deliver [payloads] to [targets]. Holds one TriggerResolver, one TargetResolver, and a list of Payloads. The auto-attack is the first one ever instantiated.

TargetResolvernew scripts/targeting/target_resolver.gd

The single-issue contract you ratified: one question — what am I pointing to? — answered as Array[GameEntity], empty is empty. Stateless and pure: the world is passed in, so previews and enemy simulations ask against any world they like.

NearestResolvernew scripts/targeting/target_resolver.gd (inner class)

The first species: opposite-half candidates, ordered by the settled preference rule. Species live as inner classes in the base's file — the same one-file arrangement TriggerResolver uses, forced by a script-loading order constraint.

Payload / AttackPayloadnew scripts/effects/payload.gd

A payload is the small dumb "what happens" species. AttackPayload is the first: it owns the strike's delivery rules and proposes the damage through the arbitration Resolver. Nothing above the payload knows attacks are special — the settled decoupling.

Mutatornew scripts/effects/mutator.gd

The contract we settled today: parameterized at parse, fed at delivery, derives on the spot, emits concrete mutations. Two day-one species (inner classes, same file): LiteralMutator (a fixed number) and HolderStatMutator (reads a named stat off the holder — the auto-attack's "my attack stat").

Feednew scripts/effects/feed.gd

The dumb plate: world · holder (+ explicit allegiance anchor, because run-scope containers have no holder) · occasion (the GameEvent) · recipient. Facts only, assembled per delivery, discarded after — deliberately not named after the deleted effect_context.gd, whose growth into a smart object is the failure this guards against.

ActionExecutornew scripts/effects/action_executor.gd

The one piece of machinery that runs an effect once its trigger says yes: asks the TargetResolver, assembles the Feed per recipient, walks the payloads, forwards presentation records. It is the only party that ever holds all four facts at once — which is exactly why the feeding job is its.

Named effectsnew data/effects/*.json

An effect authored once, by name, in the library; containers reference it by id — the same pattern abilities and statuses already use. The auto-attack is the first family: melee_attack (this initiative), then ranged_attack, leap_attack, and the policy variants (phase 3), each a full TriggeredEffect — when I act → attack payload (my attack stat) → its resolver — differing only in target resolver and presentation dressing. A card's authoring is one line: effects: ["melee_attack"]. A card that references no attack simply doesn't attack — pacifist units become authorable. Card-unique effects can still be authored inline; both forms parse to the same structure. This reclaims the id+description shells the content strip deliberately left standing, and deletes CardData.target_policy — the policy now lives inside the referenced effect.

GameEntityexists scripts/game_entity.gd

Renamed today from StatusCarrier: the shared base of units and slots — the thing a resolution points at and statuses pin to.

TriggerResolver · GameEvent · CombatCascade · Arbitratorexists

The "when" gate, the event object, the event fan-out, and the single writer of stats — all survive as-is. The cascade's razed fire() seam is where dispatch attaches; the Arbitrator's strike math (dodge, shield, crit-after-interception) is reused untouched.

Naming ruling: the arbitration layer's Resolver is renamed Arbitrator (scripts/resolver.gd → scripts/arbitrator.gd, phase 1). "Resolver" then means only the two question-answerers — TriggerResolver (when) and TargetResolver (who) — always by their full compound names in new code.

§5What this initiative deliberately does not touch

  • Manual targeting & gestures — no manual resolver, no pick-provider. Comes with the spell rebuild.
  • Spells as activated effects — ruled today, built later; nothing here blocks it.
  • Passives and interceptors — the interception seam in the arbitration Resolver stays an inert pass-through, exactly as it is now.
  • Event magnitudes — parked until a mutator species needs one.
  • Effect re-authoring beyond the attack family — the only content this initiative authors is the named attack effects and the references to them; every other stripped effect waits for its own container's rebuild.

§6Risks, named

  • The executor is the growth point. Every future structure will lean on it; if logic that belongs to species leaks into it, we rebuild the old dispatcher. Guard: the executor never inspects what a payload or mutator is — it only serves the plate.
  • Phase 2/3 gap. Between phases, units referencing not-yet-built named attacks don't fight (loud load error). Acceptable only because it lives inside this branch — flagged so it's a known state, not a surprise.
  • Tool authoring is unverified. The save gate refuses the old schema, but whether the editors can write named-effect references hasn't been checked; phase 3 verifies before the content stamp, and if the Tool can't, that gap gets raised as its own appended decision.
  • Cue wiring (phase 4) touches the presentation layer, where regressions are visual and tests are weak — hence the explicit visual check in its exit criteria.

§8Appended decisions

Raised mid-build under the sign-off protocol. Status of each is marked.

8.0 · The ruled model: ONE crank, no attack-shaped rules — SIGNED 2026-08-12

Signature note (user): repeat triggers are sanctioned CONDITIONALLY — revisited as soon as any other effect needs a follow-up, which is expected soon.

History, kept honest: the initiative paused on a phantom "strike protocol" (dissolved — see the code-is-not-design lesson), resumed, and then a first §8.0 was coded WITHOUT signature and rebuilt the special case it was deleting (attack_action, an attack-shaped procedure hand-walking effects). That code is condemned and dies with this revision. What follows is the user's model, written BEFORE the code that will obey it.

The model, in the user's words: events trigger, effects unfold. The sequencer (CombatCascade) fires act for each unit in priority order — that IS a unit's turn, and there is no separate "now attack" step anywhere. The one dispatch inside that moment finds every effect clinging to it and unfolds each. A unit's attack is an ordinary effect, indistinguishable in the rules from any other:

  • The one dispatch. CombatCascade.fire(event, holder) — the seam the demolition left waiting — gets its real body: for each effect the holder's card carries whose trigger says yes, unfold it (ActionExecutor: resolve targets → deliver payloads → return Outcomes), let presentation attend it, sweep any deaths through the existing death path. It serves act today and every event forever; nothing in it knows what an attack is.
  • melee_attack gates itself, needing nothing structural: trigger = act, of self, plus a firing condition — {"untapped": true}, a new EffectCondition form in the condition family's own key-selected style (a plain read of the unit's existing attack_exhausted boolean at ask-time, nothing stored) — so a unit whose round action is spent still gets its act moment (poison ticks) but its attack does not unfold. No writer sets the boolean today (the tap payment died with the ability costume); the condition is kept ON by user ruling 2026-08-12 — tap returns with the signed ActivatedEffect rebuild (§7), and the content is stamped once, not twice.
  • Multi-strike is an authored repetition, because content really uses it (enemies author strikes 2–3; the stat is foldable and grantable). TriggeredEffect gains one authored slot: repeats — an evaluable value (a Mutator, default literal 1) — and the unfolding runs resolve-then-deliver that many times, stopping early when a resolution comes back empty or the holder has left play. melee_attack authors "repeats": {"kind": "holder_stat", "stat": "strikes"}. Re-resolution per repeat is what preserves the playtested rule that a flurry re-acquires — a slain victim doesn't soak the follow-ups. Any effect may repeat; nothing about it is attack-specific.
  • Presentation attends the unfolding, generically. Two surfaces on CombatPresenter, no-ops on the null presenter (simulations stay instant): action_windup(holder, effect, resolution) — before payloads land; the LIVE presenter decides what that looks like from the effect's identity (for the attack family: the melee lunge held to contact, or the ranged bolt) — and action_results(holder, effect, outcomes) — after; the live presenter reads the Outcomes (dodged, crit, shield split) and plays the numbers, the "Miss", the sidestep. The choreography itself stays on the Combat scene behind the established Callable pattern. These replace the condemned attack_approach/attack_result pair — same dressings, no longer attack-named.
  • The strike's news fires from the unfolding as signed in §2: attack, struck, then dodge or crit as the Outcome reports, per delivery to a unit recipient. Emission: the dispatcher emits the news AS RESULTS, reading the returned Outcomes — the user's standing ruling ("the event dispatch is a RESULT, not a cause"); a DAMAGE-form Outcome IS the fact "a blow happened", so no payload plumbing and no attack-type inspection exists anywhere. (An earlier draft of this bullet placed emission "with the payload's delivery" — corrected at build time: that wording contradicted both the rejected payload→pipeline plumbing and the ruling above; flagged to the user in the build report.)
  • Total cleanse (user ruling, 2026-08-12), extended to my own unsigned code: already deleted — the old interleaved attack flow (_resolve_attack internals / _perform_strike / _apply_attack_damage), the hand-called per-attack StatusEngine.advance, the old named-effects tier (data + Tool tab + validator), CardData.target_policy. Dies with this revision: CombatCascade.attack_action and everything shaped like it. No remnant, no compat, no commentary survives.

8.1 · Attack is a game MECHANIC, decoupled by a FRONT DOOR — SUPERSEDED by the strike-seam pitch (see §8.0)

Phase 1 landed clean (suite 858/0, committed). Wiring phase 2 surfaced a question the flow diagram left open, and its answer went through three wrong drafts (an event-plumbing scheme rejected at review; an engine extraction that moved working code nobody asked to move) before the user's ruling landed on the simple truth:

The attack flow already works — its only problem is being rigged into the combat loop. So it is decoupled by giving it a front door, not by moving its furniture:

  • The flow stays byte-for-byte where it is — combat's strike sequence (swing choreography, Arbitrator proposal, event announcements, cues), exactly as playtested. Nothing relocates, no new plumbing exists.
  • It runs ON COMMAND: one entry point — combat's attack routine invoked with an attacker — instead of steps inlined into the loop. Today the act moment is its only caller; a future "counter-attack on dodge" payload is simply a second caller through the same door.
  • The authored effect supplies the three answers the razed layer used to: whether the unit attacks (it references an attack effect — none means it doesn't swing), whom (the effect's TargetResolver — replacing the dead find_target), and how hard (the effect's Mutator). The payload is the authored description the door reads — it executes nothing.
  • Vocabulary (ratified): "attack" is the number (the stat and its namesakes); "strike" is the deed (one blow).
  • StrikeEngine is the ratified destination, deferred: the mechanic's one home gets extracted when a second invoker actually exists — the same content-driven rule every species follows. Extracting it today would move playtested code with zero callers to justify it.
  • Parked, on the record: today's flow fires the attack event before the damage commits. The events-are-post-hoc ruling suggests strike news should follow the blow; no authored content rides the old timing (the strip forgot everything), so the question is deferred until a triggered effect actually listens for attack — it must be settled before that effect is authored.
