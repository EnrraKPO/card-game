The Strike Seam

Dissolved · never signed · builds nothing Ruling: 2026-08-12 — the problem this pitch answers DID NOT EXIST Superseded by: ATTACK_SYSTEM_DESIGN.html §8
DISSOLVED (user ruling, 2026-08-12). This pitch was scaffolding around a phantom: its "strike protocol" was inferred from degraded, accreted combat code — code the cleanup exists to delete, mistaken for design. The truth is one line: attack is one damaging effect (the Arbitrator already owns dodge/shield/crit), shared across target resolvers for the attack flavors, presented as two cues — approach, then result — holding where presentation asks. Nothing here was ever signed or built. The document is kept only as the record of the lesson (see memory: code-is-not-design); the attack initiative resumed under its own signed pitch.

Designing the attack wiring exposed that combat's strike flow is a conflation of mixed authorities: one function hand-runs rules that belong to four different owners, interleaved with visuals. This refactor dissolves the conflation — each step returns to its owner — and leaves the strike as a small mechanic with a front door anything may call. It moves no behavior the player can feel; it moves who is in charge of it.

§2The shape after the refactor

The strike mechanic becomes one small, scene-free routine — the front door:

CombatCascade.strike(attacker, target, amount) → Outcome
announce the swing → propose the damage to the Arbitrator → announce the outcome (struck, then dodge or crit) → return the Outcome. That is the whole mechanic: attack is dealing damage — the Arbitrator already owns everything inside the blow (dodge, shield, crit). Deaths ride the existing death path at the call site, unchanged.

It lives on CombatCascade because the cascade is already the home of combat's rules-moments (resolve_event, broadcast, emit_kill, bury) and already carries the two things a strike needs: the world and the presenter. (StrikeEngine as a separate class remains the ratified destination if the mechanic ever grows enough to deserve its own file — the content-driven rule. Today it would be a file holding one function.)

The visuals become presenter dressings, the pattern deaths and ground procs already use — no-ops on the null presenter, so a simulated strike runs silent and instant:

New presenter surfaceWhen it playsWhat the live fight puts there
strike_windup(attacker, target) After the swing announcement, before the blow resolves The melee lunge to its apex, or the ranged bolt's flight (ghost, concealment, swing arc — moved whole from combat, the Callable pattern LivePresenter already uses for scene-heavy dressings)
strike_impact(attacker, target, outcome) The instant the Arbitrator reports Interception playback, the "CRITICAL!" flash + speed-badge glint
strike_readout(attacker, target, outcome) After the struck announcement Dodge sidestep / "Miss" / shield-then-health damage numbers, shield-break sting

The withdrawal (retreat glide, ghost teardown) stays live-side inside the windup/readout dressings — it never was a rules step. The combat log line (a debug record of the blow) moves into the mechanic, since it describes the Outcome, not the dressing.

What combat's act loop keeps: the turn order, the flurry (the strikes stat — how many blows one act delivers), and — until the attack initiative resumes — nothing else: with targeting still razed, the loop has no victim to name, so combat stays inert after this refactor. The refactor's proof is its suite, not a fight on screen.

§3Per-attack statuses: not this design's concern

Statuses that tick "per attack" (Blind and kin) tick the way every status ticks: through StatusEngine's normal event advance, on the attack event. Combat's hand-rolled StatusEngine.advance(attacker, "attack") call is deleted with the rest of the conflation. Ruled at review (2026-08-11): no individual status's timing subtleties may drive this design — if a specific status needs finer timing, that is that status's authoring problem, solved when it is re-authored, and raised then as its own question. The system is never engineered around a single effect.

§4What this refactor does NOT touch

  • The Arbitrator — dodge, shield split, interception, crit-after-interception: byte-identical.
  • Event semantics — the same announcements in the same order as today, including the swing news firing before the blow resolves. (The parked question of whether strike news should all follow the blow stays parked in the attack pitch §8.1 — it must be settled before any effect is authored to listen for attack.)
  • The death path — kill provenance, burial, bounties: untouched.
  • Targeting and effects — nothing here reads the new machinery; the attack initiative does that when it resumes.
