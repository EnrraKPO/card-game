§1The problem

Presentation decides how effects look by reading inside them — scanning their payloads (the concrete game changes an effect carries, such as "deal damage") and type-checking their targeting (combat.gd:715, combat.gd:725, card_data.gd:556).

The root cause is not discipline but accessibility: the whole effect object is handed into presentation code (combat_cascade.gd:56), its internals readable by anyone holding it. Any rule against reading is policing, and policing fails at one discretional call.

§2The principle

Presentation never asks "do I fire now?" or "what do I do now?" A system tells presentation: "you do this now." Every single time.

No effect-shaped object ever crosses into presentation. Peeking is not forbidden — it is unwritable.

§3The solution

Every effect has two presentations — appointed by the effect itself, or filled by the defaults of §4. Each is a plain name — a word, not a structure — so naming them reveals nothing of the effect's insides.

The windup is how the actor performs the act: the lunge across the board, the projectile fired, the flare on the acting card.

The contact is what this effect landing looks like on the recipients.

A windup always plays in two steps: it opens before the effect applies, and it closes after. Many windups have nothing to close with, and that is normal — the closing step is simply empty for them.

The order of events is fixed in one place, the cascade, as three consecutive lines that already exist (combat_cascade.gd:56-58): the windup opens, the rules apply the payloads, then the contact plays together with the windup's closing stepa3. The only change to those lines is their cargo: they carry the two names instead of the effect object.

  1. The windup opens — lunge in, bolt flies, or glint flares
  2. The rules apply the payloads
  3. The contact plays, together with the windup's closing stepa3

Everything else the player sees — during a strike or any other effect — follows its own happening, not this routing (Enrra's ruling, 2026-08-12). A stat mutation's result — the damage number — presents when the mutation commits. Dodge and critical are processed inside the strike itself, and the strike flow tells presentation to show them and fires their events at the moment each actually happens. No strike record exists: the outcome — the record of what a mutation did — keeps only mutation results, and nothing later re-reads it to decide what to showa2. Nothing couples any of these to the contact or the windup: timings coincide when happenings coincide, and that is all.

ProposedThe reticle that marks a struck recipient and the hit burst ride that same happening — the mutation's commit. Awaiting Enrra's ruling.a2 — rejected

§4Defaults

An effect that appoints no windup gets the shared default: the glint, the soft flare that already marks "this card's effect is firing" for every trigger today.

An effect that appoints no contact is looked up under its own name — the id it was authored with. If nothing is found under that name, the landing shows nothing extra — silently. Enrra delegated this choice; silent is the ruled behavior.a2

§5The windups and who appoints them

Three windups are proposed. Their choreography exists in the game today; what is new is that each becomes a named thing an effect can appoint.

The lunge

The melee windup. Its opening is the physical approach: the actor crosses to the target, strikes, and rebounds. Its closing is the withdrawal — the walk back home.

The bolt

The shot windup. Its opening fires a projectile from where the actor stands. It has nothing to close.

The glint

The soft windup. Its opening is the flare on the acting card. It has nothing to close.

The five attack effects appoint theirs: nearest_attack and leap_attack take the lunge; wounded_attack, tank_attack, and threat_attack take the bolt. Every other effect falls to the default glint.

Changing what any effect looks like later means changing these appointments — data, never consumer code.

§6The demolition

Everything effect-presentation related that is not a necessary part of the exact solution above is demolished in the same stroke (Enrra's ruling) — no survivors, nothing kept "for later." By name:

  • _delivers_blow (combat.gd:715) — deleted. Choreography plays because a windup is named, not because payloads were counted.
  • _fires_bolt (combat.gd:725) — deleted. The windup's name says bolt or lunge.
  • targeting_line() (card_data.gd:556) — the producer of the tooltip's one-line targeting description — deleted as it exists today. Its replacement is the open question of §7.a4 Its replacement is §7's ruled answer: the effect's own description text.
  • The outcome's strike-story flags (dodged, crit, crit_bonus_damage on the Arbitrator's outcome) — deleted (Enrra's ruling, 2026-08-12). The outcome keeps only mutation results — health reduced, shield absorbed: everything derivable from what actually happened, never from which effect did it.a2 The strike flow announces dodge and critical itself, to presentation and to events, as they happen; nothing reads flags afterward.

One visible feature dies with the demolition: today the projectile colors itself by the attacker's elements, which requires reading the attacker card. Enrra ruled the feature dropped at this scope. Projectiles fly uncolored.

§7Open questiona4 — resolved — the card description text

Card tooltips today append a one-line description of the unit's targeting ("attacks the nearest enemy"). The mechanism that produces it reads effect internals and dies in the demolition.

Where this line comes from in the new system is unresolved and awaits Enrra's ruling. A proposal to attach it to the contact was put forward and not sanctioned; the question is open.a4 — resolved

Resolved by amendment 4 (Enrra's ruling): every effect gets a text description, and that is what the card shows.

§8The names

Every new thing the solution creates, each stated in plain words first.

The two appointed fields windup_presentation contact_presentation

The two names an effect appoints are authored as two new optional fields on the effect — Enrra's naming: descriptive over short.

The effect speaking outward windup_presentation_id() contact_presentation_id()

The effect exposes each resolved name — appointed, or default — through one method apiece. No caller ever reaches in.

The presentations data file data/presentations.json

The one data file defining the presentations themselves, with a windups section and a contacts section. A windup entry is a name bound to one of the three choreographies of §5 — the file ships with exactly the three. A contact entry describes what plays on each recipient when the effect lands — and at this scope the contacts section ships empty — no effect appoints onea2. The section exists so future visuals (and §7's answer, if it lands here)a4 have their lawful place. A contact, once one exists, plays once per recipient.

The loader PresentationLibrary scripts/effects/presentation_library.gd

One loader reads that file at startup and refuses malformed or duplicate content, the same way the effect library already behaves. It answers two questions: give me the windup with this name, give me the contact with this name.

One existing structure must be understood to see where the change lands. The game can run a whole fight with no visuals — simulations do exactly that, running the real rules on invisible copies of the board. This works because the rules never speak to the screen; they speak to a presenter: one object holding the show-this-moment functions the cascade calls. Two presenters exist today: CombatPresenter, the silent one that shows nothing (simulations use it), and LivePresenter, the one live fights use, which forwards each moment to the visible combat scene through two functions Combat exposes for the purpose — those two receive the names as well.

Today, the presenter's windup and results functions are handed the whole effect object. They will receive the two presentation names instead. After this change, no presentation code in the codebase is handed an effect at all.

ProposedThe happenings of §3 need mouths, because today those visuals are produced by reading the outcome's flags after the fact — the path §6 demolishes. Awaiting Enrra's ruling on the shape and the names: the presenter grows one function per happening. strike_dodged (the sidestep and "Miss") and strike_crit (the flash) belong to the strike flow alone, called at the moment each happens; mutation_result (the number, the reticle, the hit burst) is announced by whatever commits the mutation — every mutation of every effect, strike or not, at the moment it commits. The results call keeps only what §3 gives it: the contact and the windup's close. The silent presenter answers each with nothing, as it does everything.a3 — rejected

§9Scope

This document cleanses the presentation peek only. The rest of phase 4 is untouched: the seam where the board asks which unit an attacker would strike (find_target), the annotated debts waiting in the board script (the NEEDS blocks in combat_board.gd), and giving each attack its own distinct visuals. Nothing here stands on the cursed additions of ATTACK_SYSTEM_DESIGN.html; every statement stands on the 2026-08-12 conversation alone.

Amendment · iteration 2 · 2026-08-12

Ruled by Enrra in conversation after the iteration-1 read. Where a point below contradicts the body above, the point below governs.

The outcome is ruled out of existence

A mutation commits, announces its presentation and fires its events at that moment, and returns nothing. Nothing anywhere writes down what happened for later re-reading. The arithmetic of a blow — dodge, shield split, critical — stays internal to the Arbitrator, the single writer that performs it.

Every piece of code currently holding an outcome is a conformance site to be brought to this design or demolished; the codebase has no veto over the design.

Supersedes §3's "the outcome keeps only mutation results" and §6's outcome bullet.

Events fire at commit

The events of an effect mutation fire at mutation time, from the committing site — never derived from a record afterward. Dodge and critical still announce from the strike flow, as §3 states.

Completes the ruling above: supersedes §3's "the outcome — the record of what a mutation did — keeps only mutation results" for events as well as for presentation.

The mark and the burst are contact presentation

§3's proposed container is resolved — rejected as written. The mark that snaps onto a recipient and the hit burst are not universal mutation announcements. They are contact presentation — the landing look §3 already defines — routed like any contact. Housekeeping mutations (shield regeneration and its kin) have no contact and show nothing.

Vocabulary ruling: that mark is the recipient mark — never "reticle," never "target"-anything; it plays at the landing and has nothing to do with targeting.

Resolves §3's PROPOSED container.

The default contact exists

The contacts section ships with ONE entry — the default landing: the recipient mark and the hit burst. An effect that appoints no contact and has no entry under its own name falls to the default landing, exactly as the windup slot falls to the glint.

Proposed the entry's id in data: default_landing.

Supersedes §4's contact silence and §8's "ships empty."

§8's proposed mouths adjust accordinglya3

mutation_result announces the number alone — the recipient mark and the burst belong to the contact — and with the outcome gone, no record exists for anything to read.a3

Adjusts §8's PROPOSED container. The mouths themselves were later rejected whole — see amendment 3.

Amendment · iteration 3 · 2026-08-12

Ruled by Enrra in conversation after the iteration-2 read. Where a ruling below contradicts anything above, the ruling below governs. Scratched text throughout the page marks statements these amendments invalidated — scratched, never deleted, each tagged with the amendment that retired it (a2, a3).

The windup's closure is its own telling

The contact and the windup's closing step are two different happenings, and each is told at its own moment: the effect landing tells the contact — "the effect lands: contact, now" — and the act concluding tells the windup to close — "the act concludes: close your windup, now." They usually come one on the other's heels, so the eye sees what today's game shows — but that is coincidence of timing under §3's own rule, not a bundle. No instruction carries them together.

Supersedes "together with the windup's closing step" in §3's prose and its step list.

One generic channel — the per-happening interface is rejected

§8's proposed container is resolved — rejected whole. A dodge's sidestep, a critical's flash, a damage number: each is simply a visual — something to show, on someone, now, with a magnitude when there is one. The game already owns the machinery for exactly that: the VFX library, where named visuals live as data.

So presentation is told through one generic channel: a named visual, a recipient, a magnitude when there is one. The strike flow tells it "show dodge on this unit"; a mutation's commit tells it "show damage_number here, amount 3." What each name looks like lives in the VFX data — if X, do whatever X maps to — and a new happening is a new data entry, never a new function. Silence for simulations is one gate on the one channel.

Rejects §8's PROPOSED container (the three per-happening functions) and retires amendment 2's "mutation_result announces the number alone" — the number is a named visual through the same channel.

Amendment · iteration 4 · 2026-08-12

Ruled by Enrra in conversation after the iteration-3 read. Where a ruling below contradicts anything above, the ruling below governs.

§7 is resolved — every effect has a text description, and the card shows it

Every effect gets a text description. That is what the card shows: the tooltip's line is each effect's own text, presented as given — the card derives nothing from the effect's insides.

The description's home is the locale table, under a key derived from the effect's identity: effect.<id>.desc — e.g. effect.nearest_attack.desc. No new field on the effect; the id is the key, and the text is translated alongside every other string. Keying by the effect's identity — its id as an opaque token, never its contents — was ruled lawful routing in the same design conversation that ruled the seal; the effect's contents stay unread.

Entailed by the ruling: the five existing strings at targeting.<policy>.desc (en and es) move to their effect-identity keys, and the targeting.* keys die with targeting_line(). The answer does not land in the contacts section of data/presentations.json — a description is text on the card, not a landing visual.

Resolves §7 whole; supersedes the ribbon's "one open question," §6's "open question of §7," and §8's "§7's answer, if it lands here."
