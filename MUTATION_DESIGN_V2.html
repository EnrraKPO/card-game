Design pitch · the effect system

The Mutation of Facts II

Pitch — unsigned Drafted: 2026-08-16 Branch: nuke-channel Supersedes: MUTATION_DESIGN.html (retired to record)

The write system: how the game changes its facts. An effect carries a trigger (when) and a target resolver (whom); this pitch establishes the third slot (what) — and the machinery every write in the game travels, effect-borne and machinery- borne alike.

§1 The system at a glance

a payload delivers  →  each mutator issues  →  the MutationEngine dispatches  →  procedures resolve  →  the WriteAuthority commits  →  events return to the issuer  →  the payload's holster gathers them  →  the cascade fires the holster

One effect firing, end to end:

  1. The cascade catches a trigger (or the activation entrance is invoked) and hands the effect to its EffectConductor.
  2. The conductor resolves the effect's targets, cues the windup, and engages every mutator of the effect's payload, handing each a per-recipient plate of facts.
  3. Each mutator reasons freely over its plate and submits zero or more EngineRequests to the MutationEngine.
  4. The engine hands each request to the procedure that claims its shape; the procedure resolves it — applying lower procedures where their happenings happen — into writes committed through the WriteAuthority. Submission is synchronous and returns the mutation's events, which the conductor collects for the delivery's holster (§9).
  5. The conductor cues the contact and the conclusion. The cascade then fires the payload's holster (§9).

The audience: the cascade polls every effect holder that exists in its world — fielded entities, slots' and units' statuses, the run's holders (relics, upgrades), and cards in hand. Every effect carries an implied default condition — the holder is not a unit sitting in hand — which an authored form may explicitly remove; a hand-held card's effects fire only when it does. (The removal's syntax belongs to the markup shape, §14.4.)

§2 The payload

The effect's anatomy is a triad: trigger = when · targets = whom · payload = what. The payload is a bare typed list on the effect — a bundle of mutators delivered together:

var payload: Array[Mutator]

There is no payload wrapper type: parse validation belongs to the effect's parser, delivery belongs to the conductor, and the list itself has no behavior. One delivery of a payload is the unit of issuance and the scope of the holster (§9). An effect bears its payload; machinery hands the cascade a bare list on the spot.

§3 The mutator

A mutator is the game's asking voice: a content-nameable object that turns an intent — authored by content or constructed by machinery — into concrete requests, using the facts of the firing. A mutator is the only thing that issues requests. One ask is one request, one mutation, one provenance — the mutator's identity.

  • Internals are unbounded code. A mutator may read anything and reason however it likes before speaking. The strike mutator asks that the recipient be struck; the draw mutator reads the deck's top from the world and asks that the card move to hand. Markup stays flat, and a new capability is a new mutator in code, never new authoring vocabulary.
  • Contract: issue(plate) → events. Invoked by the conductor; submits zero, one, or several requests and returns the events those mutations produced.
  • Mutator-to-procedure is many-to-few. Differently-reasoning mutators may all conclude in the same request type.
  • The roster is the game's: a mutator exists for each way the game asks for change. Content names an effect's payload mutators; machinery constructs its own in code.

§4 The plate

Because mutator internals are unbounded, mutator inputs cannot be enumerated. Instead, every mutator receives the same plate of facts at issuance — one uniform signature that never grows. One fact is the world itself: every question about game state is answerable through it, which is what lets the plate stay closed. The remaining facts are situational — the circumstances of this issuance, which the world cannot supply: who holds the effect, on what occasion it fired, whom this delivery lands on.

§5 The EngineRequest

What a mutator issues, and what the engine receives. The facts changing is the mutation; this object is the asking — minting a request is the act of asking, and only mutators mint. It is a dumb data record: a base class carrying the context bundle common to every request, and one subclass per procedure, named by the happening it asks for, carrying that procedure's parameters as typed members.

class_name EngineRequest          # the base: the context bundle
    var mutator: Mutator          # who asked — identity IS the provenance
    var source: EffectHolder GameEntity  # the holder it came from (§11, as amended)
    var target: GameEntity        # whom it addresses; null = no target entity exists

class StatMutationRequest extends EngineRequest:
    var stat: StringName
    var delta: int

class StrikeRequest extends EngineRequest:
    pass                          # the striker is the source; the strike procedure
                                  #   reads its attack (§6)

class DamageRequest extends EngineRequest:
    var amount: int
  • The subclass type is the verb. No kind field, no parameter dictionary, no schema shared across verbs.
  • The parameter contract has two parties: the mutators that speak the verb fill the members; the procedure that claims it reads them. The engine transports without reading; conditions interrogate only the context bundle.
  • A request is complete at construction. Context is stamped at issuance and travels unmodified; a null context slot states that the party does not exist.
  • The context bundle is a floor. A request may carry more than the three slots; the extension mechanism is future work (§13).

§6 The MutationEngine and its procedures

The MutationEngine is the single entry point for every mutation in the game. The doorway is MutationEngine.submit(request) — static, callable from anywhere; synchronous, returning the mutation's events. Requests are self-sufficient, so one doorway serves the live fight and every simulated world.

A procedure is the game's way of resolving one kind of happening into writes and events. A procedure is a fixed part of the engine, stateless — all state lives in the world's facts — and owns one request shape. Its signature is its typed parameters plus the originating request as context; its events are stamped from that context. In code, the base class is EngineProcedure; each concrete procedure is named by the happening it resolves, and its request pairs with it by that name.

  • The engine has its procedures fixed in code, as a class has its parts. Its one decision is handing each request to the procedure that claims its shape; every other decision is made before submission, by a mutator, or during resolution, by a procedure. A new happening enters as one new procedure and its request; nothing existing changes.
  • A procedure has two entrances, one body. Asked: a mutator issues the procedure's request; the procedure unpacks it and runs. Applied: a procedure, mid-resolution, runs a lower procedure directly, passing the parameters it computed and the request already in its hand. Both entrances run the same body, produce the same events, stamp the same way.
  • Application stays inside the one mutation. No request is minted on the applied path; no new ask originates inside a resolution. The applying procedure's request travels down as context, so provenance stays singular: a burn's damage carries the burn mutator, a strike's damage carries the strike mutator, and the damaged event is produced identically on either path.
  • A procedure applies a procedure only when the applied happening happens. Damage happens inside a connecting strike, so the strike procedure applies the damage procedure. Where only a raw write happens, a procedure uses a WriteAuthority primitive (§7): spawn docks a unit — no board move happens.
  • Every decision lives in a procedure; a primitive never decides. A procedure may hold much law, little, or none.

§7 The WriteAuthority

Every change to a game fact goes through the WriteAuthority. No bypass, from any origin. It performs writes and decides nothing.

  • Its primitives comprise the stat write and the structural writes — dock (occupy a board cell), undock (vacate it), container insert, container remove, mint — each existing exactly once (exact roster: §14.13).
  • It owns each fact's arithmetic — floors, caps, whatever governs a specific fact's committed value — in exactly one place. Arithmetic bounds the write's performance; it is not a decision.
  • It returns the fact events of each write — the crossings it detected: health reaching zero is died, shield reaching zero is shield depleted — as bare, context-free facts. One author per fact event, blind to which procedure drove the write, so every procedure present or future produces them correctly by construction.
  • The authority is the hardening point. Emerging consequences of facts are written into it with relative freedom; it is monolithic at this scope, and its internal structure is deferred until its contents demand one.

§8 The EffectConductor

The owner of what running one effect means. The triad's parts each answer one question — when, whom, what — and hold no flow; the conductor asks them in order and tells presentation between the steps. It is built by the cascade from the cascade's own (world, presenter) pair, at construction.

The run, exactly:

  1. Resolve the effect's targets against the world. An empty resolution ends the run; it is a legal no-target.
  2. Cue the windup (the effect's windup presentation name, the recipients).
  3. Deliver: engage every payload mutator on every recipient's plate (nesting order — §14.7). Requests flow to the engine as they are issued.
  4. Cue the contact (the effect's contact presentation name, the recipients).
  5. Cue the conclusion (the windup name closes).

The conductor ends at step 5. After it returns, the cascade fires the payload's holster (§9).

Two entrances, one conductor: triggered dispatch enters through the cascade's event machinery; the activation path (effects with a cost instead of a trigger — abilities, consumables) enters through a cascade entrance that skips audience-and-trigger and invokes the same conductor.

§9 The act and the holster

An act is one payload delivery. Within one act, everything commits as it happens; between acts, the world responds. The resolution was read at the trigger moment and delivers whole. Facts change instantly mid-act — a killed unit is dead that instant — but reactions open at the act boundary: three units killed by one blow died simultaneously, whatever order their requests committed in.

The mutations of one payload are simultaneous for the purpose of event firing. No event is dispatched at its own commit. Events wait in the holster — the payload's container of pending events — and fire together once the payload's last mutation has resolved. One holster per payload delivery, owned by the cascade: the cascade opens the delivery and creates the holster; after the conductor returns, the cascade fires the holster's events in order; the holster dies when its last event has fired and its consequences have unfolded. Nothing persists between deliveries.

Firing is depth-first. An event may trigger a fresh effect, which resolves in full — its own payload, its own holster — before the next event of the outer holster fires.

A death event is an ordinary holstered event, fired and reacted to like any other. Death carries one innate consequence the game itself executes: when a death event's reactions have unfolded, the cascade executes the burial — removal from the board.

§10 Events

An event born of a request has two halves:

  • The carried half — the request's context bundle (mutator, source, target), forwarded with every event the request gives rise to. Provenance is the mutator's identity; there is no separate provenance vocabulary.
  • The produced half — what actually happened at commit: died, shield emptied, health lost. Fact events are authored by the WriteAuthority; procedure events (struck, dodged) are authored by the resolving procedure. "Died from poison" reads both halves.

Events travel by return. The WriteAuthority returns the fact events of each write; a procedure returns those together with its own events, stamped from the request in its hand; the doorway returns the mutation's events to the submitter; the mutator returns them from issue(); the conductor collects them into the delivery's holster. Events are holstered at the payload scope alone.

Effect boundaries restart the context. When an event triggers another effect, that effect's requests carry their own context; context never accumulates across effects.

§11 The effect holder

One common base class for everything that holds effects. Holding effects, querying them, being queued for resolution, and riding in a request's source slot are type-agnostic: the plumbing traffics in the base class. Concrete types reappear only in condition evaluation, via checked downcast.

  • The hierarchy: the effect-holder base is the root; GameEntity derives from it (units, board slots, sides); relics and statuses derive from it directly. Amended 2026-08-16 GameEntity IS the root: holding effects and bearing stats couple there. Relics, statuses, and sides are GameEntities directly. Slots thereby hold effects — dormant capacity for future boards whose slots act. A side is an entity: mana lives on the side as a stat (§6), and a side holds effects and statuses — the same dormant capacity, for side-wide play.   • Everything is typed. The house law: no Variant members, no inferred Variant.
  • Cost, accepted: the base's permanent claim on the hierarchy root — every future holder kind descends from it.

§12 Conditions interrogate the source

The source slot carries the actual issuing object, and trigger conditions are predicates asked of it. "Was the death caused by me" is an identity comparison; "was the source a fire unit" is a checked downcast plus a property read. A failed downcast is a clean false: a relic-sourced death is not "caused by a fire unit." A real object answers any predicate written against it, present or future, with nothing added to the request's shape.

§13 Out of frame

Named so absence is never read as oversight:

  • Interception.
  • The context-bundle extension mechanism (§5).
  • The procedures' internal arithmetic — dodge odds, crit numbers — each arrives by its own pitch; §6 fixes where they live (the strike procedure), not what they compute.
  • The authored markup shape (but see §14.4 — it gates implementation).

§14 Open questions

Each is ruled, explicitly deferred, or dismissed as safe-to-infer before code touches its ground.

First level

1. Null semantics. Ratify: null in a context slot means the party genuinely does not exist — never "unknown." Suggested: ratify as stated.

2. The plate's name and roster (§4). The occasions on which machinery issues — outside any effect's firing — fold into this roster. Suggested: the name stays Plate; roster = the world, the holder, the occasion (the event that fired the effect), the recipient (whom this delivery lands on). Machinery fills the same four slots, null where the party does not exist.

3. The effect-holder base class's name (§11). Suggested: EffectHolder — §5's request sketch already speaks it. Ruled 2026-08-16 Dissolved by the §11 amendment. There is no EffectHolder to name: GameEntity is the root (effects + stats); BoardEntity derives (board presence + statuses).

Second level

4. The authored markup shape. Needed on day one: effect files must name their mutators in some syntax before anything runs. Suggested: a "payload" array on the effect; each entry names one mutator — string shorthand where the mutator takes no authored parameters ("strike"), an object naming the mutator plus its typed parameters where it does ({"stat_mutation": {"stat": "speed", "delta": 3}}); the parser refuses strangers.

5. The event roster's names. Fact events belong to the WriteAuthority, procedure events to each procedure; the names are settled fact-by-fact and procedure-by-procedure. Suggested: past-tense happening verbs — fact events died, shield_depleted; procedure events struck, dodged, crit, damaged, then moved/spawned/buried/drawn as their procedures build.

6. Procedure-to-presentation announcements. Per-commit visuals (damage number, shield flash) come from the resolving side; how the announcement travels and what it may say. Suggested: procedures announce at commit through the established presentation channel — named visual, recipient, magnitude — with simulation silence by the channel's existing gate. Ruled 2026-08-16 Announcements are gathered, not performed. A procedure announcing at commit records what happened — named visual, recipient, magnitude — into the act's account; nothing plays mid-resolution and the engine never waits. After the payload's effects have resolved, the presenter receives the whole account at the flow's presentation beat and runs it at its own will — merging, sequencing, and pacing its cues with full authority — and any VFX determines the span of its block before greenlighting the flow. Simulation silence stays with the channel's existing gate.

Third level

7. The delivery nesting order (§8 step 3) — per-recipient-then-per-mutator or the reverse; the two orders read different worlds mid-act. The unit under question is one payload delivery. Suggested: per-mutator-then-per-recipient — the payload list is the authored sequence, so its order dominates: each mutator speaks its full ask across all recipients before the next mutator begins. Ruled 2026-08-16 Suggestion accepted as stated.

8. Delivery onto the mid-act dead. A recipient may already be a corpse when its delivery arrives. Deliver anyway, or skip? Suggested: deliver — §9 already rules the resolution was read at the trigger moment and delivers whole; a corpse absorbs its delivery, and burial waits at the boundary. Skipping would be special-casing inside the act. Ruled 2026-08-16 Deliver, on the simultaneity ground. The commit sequence inside an act is mechanical bookkeeping, not game-time: within one act no recipient is ever "already dead" for delivery purposes — the unit becomes a corpse as the act resolves, by the act's totality. Skipping would fabricate a before-and-after the design denies. Mid-walk reads do see earlier commits (open 7 owns the walk order), but no decision about delivery may hang on the sequence. Burial waits at the boundary.

9. The doorway's refusal discipline. A request no procedure claims, or a null target where the verb requires one: loud error, silent drop, or impossible by construction. Suggested: loud error, with the type system closing what it can first — never a silent drop.

10. The carried half of request-less events. Phase markers (turn start, act) are born from no request. One event shape with honest nulls, or two acknowledged kinds. Suggested: one event shape with honest nulls — the same null discipline as open 1, and no second event vocabulary.

11. The WriteAuthority's form — static facility or owned instance. Suggested: static, matching the engine — requests are self-sufficient and all state lives in the world's facts, so one authority serves the live fight and every simulated world.

12. Plain changes versus crossings. Whether every write produces a fact event, or only threshold crossings do and plain changes are served by presentation announcements alone (adjoins open 6). Suggested: crossings only — plain changes announce to presentation and produce no fact event until content demands a trigger on one. Ruled 2026-08-16 Need is the axis, not the crossing. A fact event exists only where content actually cares; none is issued ahead of need. Any fact mutation — crossing or plain change — may bear an event the moment something cares about it; nothing bears one before. Shield depleted is hypothetical: unfired today, at most standable-up early as the exemplar of the shape. Presentation is served regardless, through the act's account (open 6).

13. The WriteAuthority's primitive roster — the exact list of primitives and their signatures. Suggested: exactly §7's list — the stat write, dock, undock, container insert, container remove, mint — growing only when a procedure needs a decision-free write that no primitive covers. Ruled 2026-08-16 Exactly the six, with container widened: a container is any coordinate-less collection of world objects — deck, hand, graveyard, and a BoardEntity's statuses alike. The six read as an ontology of world structure: a number (the stat write), a board occupancy (dock, undock), a membership (container insert, container remove), an existence (mint). A status application is mint + insert into the holder's statuses; cleanse and expiry are container removes; stacks and ticks are stat writes on the status object (statuses bear stats as GameEntities, §11 as amended). Per-container law stays above the primitives, in procedures. Signatures are pinned when procedures are written against them; the growth rule stands as suggested.

14. The mutator's lifecycle. Who constructs mutators, when, and whether they are stateless immutable instances shared across card copies and simulated worlds. Suggested: stateless immutable instances — constructed at parse for the effect habitat, on the spot by machinery — shared safely across card copies and worlds, since every situational fact arrives on the plate. Ruled 2026-08-16 Suggestion accepted as stated. A mutator holds only its authored parameters, never accumulates state across firings; provenance identifies the ask as authored, while the request's source slot answers "whose"; anything needing memory is a world fact.
